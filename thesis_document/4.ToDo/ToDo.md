- Motivation of my thesis: Classical DUE models typically assume homogeneous and perfectly rational agents. However, real transportation systems contain substantial behavioral heterogeneity, including drivers with imperfect information, habitual route preferences, different sensitivities to congestion, or bounded rationality. Due to the highly nonlinear nature of congestion dynamics, even a relatively small proportion of such agents may significantly alter the resulting traffic patterns. Therefore, studying decentralized reinforcement-learning-based traffic assignment provides a useful framework for analyzing how heterogeneous and adaptive driver behaviors collectively shape network-level traffic dynamics.
